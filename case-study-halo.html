Halo Level Design

Building a map for 2v2 custom matches.

User Research Prototyping Level Design Figma

The problem

We were playing 2v2 on maps that were designed for 4v4

Playing 4 player split screen means you have a quarter of the viewport that you'd ordinarily have. Combine that with playing 2v2 on Halo's official maps that are designed for 4v4 meant our games were... slow.

I tested this by measuring our experience, to the intended baseline.

4v4 baseline

Avg match time 318s
Avg time to find encounter 4.8s

2v2 custom match

Avg match time 657s
Avg time to find encounter 9.5s

The process

1

Research

Studied popular Halo maps to understand what makes a good arena layout work at different player counts.

2

Design

Sketched five different map concepts on paper, each with a different approach to condensing sightlines and routes.

3

Prototype

Built each sketch as a low-fidelity blockout in Halo's Forge editor, wireframed in Figma.

4

Test

Playtested prototypes with AI bots and measured time-to-encounter against the 4v4 baseline.

5

Iterate

Refined the best-performing design through multiple rounds, adjusting routes and spawn points each time.

The surprise

Playtesting with real people gave me crucial feedback

Two pieces of feedback surprised me:

Weapon sandbox. Players didn't recognise the newer weapons I'd placed on the map, and didn't know how they worked. I swapped everything for classic weapons they already knew.

Visual hierarchy. On a quarter of the screen, the map was hard to read. Key routes didn't stand out. I used lighting and colour contrast to create clearer navigational cues so players could tell where to go at a glance.
